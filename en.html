Case Study 01 · February Tết Promotion

“Prosperous Spring, Perfect Gifts”

A Lunar New Year promotion rolled out simultaneously across the Cheers chain, using a 100%-win scratch card mechanic to lift average basket value.

Timeframe
01/02 – 16/02 16 days
Scope Coordinated
20 stores across the Cheers chain
Role
Operations lead planning · POSM · reporting

01 Programme Mechanics

Customers with a receipt of VND 79,000 or more received one scratch card, limited to one per receipt. The VND 79,000 threshold was set to nudge customers into adding an item to qualify. The prize pool was split into three tiers, all redeemed instantly at the counter:

  • Tết edition red envelope pack the core prize, matched to seasonal demand
  • Surprise gift wind chimes and night lights, supplier-sponsored
  • Exclusive Lô Tô board game set the top prize, a collectible for the Tết season

02 Scratch Card Artwork

An 8×6cm scratch card set in four designs, produced and distributed to each store with a signed quantity receipt.

03 Measured Results

10.569 qualifying receipts
VND 2.087bn revenue recorded
1,15% cost as share of revenue
88,07% scratch card distribution rate
Gift allocation and redemption by tier
Prize tier Allocated Redeemed Rate
Red envelope pack 1.724 1.585 91,9%
Lô Tô board game set 94 85 90,4%
Surprise gift 972 818 84,2%
Total scratch cards 2.825 2.488 88,1%

All three prize tiers moved at comparable rates with no surplus left over, showing the prize mix was allocated sensibly at the planning stage.

04 Standout Results

VND 2.09bn revenue over the 16-day run

10,569 qualifying receipts in just 16 days — the VND 79,000 threshold did exactly its job of lifting basket value, generating VND 2.087 billion in revenue at a programme cost of just 1.15%.

An even 84–92% redemption rate across all three tiers indicates the prize mix matched customer tastes over Tết; 12 of 20 stores cleared 100% of their allocation.

Room for improvement: gift allocation did not closely track actual demand at each store cluster — some stores ran out early while others were left with stock. This is the basis for the recommendations below.

05 Recommendations for the Next Cycle

  1. Forecast store-level gift demand from historical data

    Use receipt data from previous cycles as the basis for allocating gift volumes per store, so stock prepared tracks each outlet's real demand.

  2. Segment stores to steer budget allocation

    Group outlets into three tiers by customer profile and seasonality, with a dedicated allocation for each; prioritise the hospital and residential clusters, where revenue is steady and rises over Tết.

  3. Standardise the counter conversation

    Train staff on a short script to invite customers to top up to the qualifying threshold, and track redemption by store so promotional stock can be replenished in time.

06 In-Store Communication Assets

An A4 mechanics sheet placed at the checkout counter as a tool for staff to explain the offer at the point of payment. Designed and distributed to all 20 outlets before launch day.

Key Takeaways

The core problem here was allocating finite resources across many stores with different customer behaviour, then measuring the outcome to calibrate the next cycle. Three capabilities show through directly:

  • Performance tracking at unit level: reading data store by store rather than stopping at the aggregate, which is what makes timely reallocation between outlets possible.
  • Behavioural analysis by customer segment: identifying seasonality and local characteristics to plan allocation that suits each store cluster.
  • Clear recommendations and direction: each item comes with a specific improvement target and a way to track it next cycle.
Case Study 02 · New Store Opening Week

“Opening Day Cheer, Gifts All Round”

Opening week for a new store in an apartment complex: four gift mechanics running in parallel over seven days to draw residents in to shop from week one.

Timeframe
28/06 – 05/07 first 7 days of trading
Scope Coordinated
1 new store in an apartment complex
Role
Operations lead planning · POSM · gifting

01 Four Mechanics Running in Parallel

Rather than a single offer, the event used four mechanics aimed at four different first-time shopper behaviours — from a small trial purchase to a longer-term commitment:

  • Opening gift — receipts from VND 99,000 a back cushion (supplier-sponsored), 300 units
  • Lucky gift bag — receipts from VND 200,000 five household essentials, 500 units
  • Membership sign-up a glass tumbler — converting walk-in shoppers into members on the spot
  • Receipt collection — premium reward collect multiple receipts to claim a premium non-stick pan

02 Event Photos

03 Measured Results

4 mechanics running in parallel
790 gifts handed out over 7 days
95,2% redemption against total allocation
5,7% cost as share of revenue

Revenue over the first seven days reached VND 48.1 million at a brand-new outlet, with gift cost at just 5.7%. Both instant-reward mechanics cleared 100% of allocation, confirming the gift mix suited the tastes of the apartment-complex customer base.

04 Standout Results

100% of gift allocation cleared during opening week

Both the VND 99,000 opening gift and the membership sign-up gift cleared 100% of allocation. The VND 200,000 gift bag reached 92% — the core reward tier, and the one that directly lifted basket value among first-time shoppers.

The result confirms that instant reward at the counter is the right mechanic for the opening phase, when new customers need an immediate positive experience to bring them back in the following weeks.

Room for improvement: a mechanic requiring customers to collect multiple receipts does not suit the opening phase, when the habit of returning has not yet formed. This is the basis for the adjustment recommended below.

05 Recommendations for the Next Cycle

  1. Prioritise instant-reward mechanics during the opening phase

    Concentrate budget on gifts handed over at the counter during opening week, and introduce collection-based programmes from week three, once a regular shopping habit has formed.

  2. Expand the reward tier that matches buying behaviour

    Increase the allocation for the VND 200,000 tier — it cleared 92% and directly lifts average basket value.

  3. Follow up with a resident-only programme

    After the opening, move to recurring offers reserved for complex residents (delivery to the apartment door, a fixed weekly offer day) to turn first-time visitors into regulars.

Key Takeaways

At its core this case is about running several promotional mechanics in parallel within one event and measuring each of them separately alongside the 95.2% aggregate:

  • Measuring each mechanic separately: tracking each mechanic alongside the total — a habit that transfers directly to monitoring platform-level KPIs when several channels run at once.
  • Matching mechanic to channel lifecycle: each stage in an outlet's life suits a different type of mechanic — a principle that applies to any newly opened channel, physical store or online storefront alike.
  • Allocation decisions grounded in data: every proposed budget adjustment is backed by figures measured at the event itself.
Case Study 03 · Reward Mechanic Comparison

Instant Win versus Deferred Draw

Two gamification campaigns run across the same store chain, differing in one core variable — when the customer receives the reward — producing a more than tenfold gap in participation.

Campaign A
Deferred prize draw “Summer Festival” · 14 days
Campaign B
100%-win instant draw “Deal & Chill” · 18 days
Role
Operations lead, both planning · execution · reporting

01 The Two Mechanics Compared

Both used the same model — reach the receipt threshold, receive an entry code — but the experience after receiving the code was entirely different:

  • A · Deferred result customers registered their details and entered an end-of-period draw for cinema tickets — few prizes, high value, delivered as e-vouchers
  • B · 100% instant win customers drew and received a gift on the spot — plush toys, drink samples, arm-rest cushions, most of them supplier-sponsored

02 Artwork for Both Campaigns

03 Comparison Results

7.400 customer entries over 18 days
39,6% chain-wide participation, instant-win mechanic
×10,7 versus the comparison mechanic run previously
≤0,3% cost as share of revenue, both campaigns
Instant-win participation by store cluster
Store cluster Participation rate
Dormitories & student areas 23,6 – 67,6%
Hospital cluster 25,2 – 39,2%
Apartments & residential 18,2 – 52,2%
Chain-wide 39,6%

Both campaigns were highly cost-efficient, with prize budgets at just 0.15% and 0.3% of revenue. The digital draw also carried a real operational advantage: rewards issued as e-vouchers need no display space, no stock management and no shrinkage of physical gifts in store.

04 What the Campaign Revealed

×10,7 participation when the reward is instant

The instant-win mechanic reached 39.6% participation chain-wide — more than ten times the comparison mechanic run previously — and 67.6% at the leading store. The gap held consistently across all three store clusters, indicating that reward timing is the decisive variable for participation.

A second finding: how well the prize fits the audience determines who takes part. With cinema tickets as the draw prize, stores in student areas saw markedly higher participation than others because the reward matched what younger shoppers want — grounds for tailoring the prize mix to each store cluster in future cycles.

Room for improvement: applying a single prize mix across the whole chain meant customers outside the target segment largely ignored the programme. This is the basis for the recommendations below.

05 Recommendations for the Next Cycle

  1. Make instant win the default for short-run demand pushes

    Reserve deferred draws for high-value, low-volume grand prizes; move every programme aimed at lifting purchase frequency over two to three weeks onto instant reward at the counter.

  2. Tailor prizes to each store cluster

    Student clusters favour cinema tickets, which is why their participation runs well ahead of other outlets; hospital and residential clusters respond better to shopping vouchers and everyday essentials — same budget, but each audience gets the reward that suits them.

  3. Set a code-scan KPI for each store

    Run a store league table on participation rate with spot bonuses for cashiers, turning the person at the counter into an active communication channel rather than an overlooked link.

Key Takeaways

This is, in substance, a controlled comparison of mechanics across two campaign cycles, read against the same customer frame of reference:

  • Controlled comparison: setting two campaigns side by side cluster by cluster rather than comparing totals — an approach that applies directly to comparing the same programme across different platforms.
  • Isolating variables: separating the effect of the mechanic from the effect of the prize — the basis for designing the next programme deliberately rather than repeating out of habit.
  • Judging mechanics on more than one axis: comparing two mechanics on operating cost and effort as well as participation — e-voucher rewards, for instance, spare stores from displaying, storing and counting physical gifts.
Case Study 04 · Gamification & Vendor Management

“Ice Cream & Milk Festival”

A campaign bringing an online game into stores across the Co.opSmile chain: customers buying from the target category received a code and entered it on a web game to spin for prizes. I sourced and contracted the platform vendor myself, then tracked the data down to store level.

Timeframe
15/05 – 28/05 14 days
Scope Coordinated
133 stores across the Co.opSmile chain
Role
Operations lead vendor selection & contract · rollout · reporting

01 Programme Mechanics

Customers buying from the ice cream and dairy category with a receipt of VND 59,000 or more received one entry code printed on the receipt; receipts from VND 118,000 received two. Codes were entered on a web game and played on the phone directly, with no app to install.

  • Wheeled travel suitcase 10 prizes, supplied by the sponsoring brand
  • Children's bicycle 10 prizes, aimed at family shoppers
  • Power bank 10 prizes, aimed at younger shoppers

All 30 prizes were sponsored by the brand — the chain ran the programme at zero gift cost.

02 The Game Platform

The chain had no gamification tool in place. I sourced, compared and selected an external platform vendor, negotiated pricing and contract terms, then pulled the platform data back and reconciled it store by store for the report.

03 Measured Results

VND 581.3M category revenue over 14 days
9.852 entry codes issued to customers
33,2% of codes activated in the game
VND 0 gift cost borne by the chain

3,272 customers went to the web game of their own accord and left contact details — a direct engagement channel that a conventional counter promotion simply does not create.

04 What the Campaign Revealed

0–95% spread in participation rate between stores

Same programme, same prize structure, yet participation across stores ranged from 0% to 95%. The leading group hit 70–95%; the bottom group fell below 10%, with some outlets activating no codes at all.

That spread shows the success of a digital programme depends more on execution at the counter than on the mechanic itself. Where cashiers actively introduced it, participation was very high; where they did not, codes were printed and thrown away.

Two Barriers Identified
  • Execution

    In low-performing stores, cashiers did not explain the benefit or how to take part. Codes were printed as normal, but customers did not know what to do with them.

  • Friction

    The process asked customers to take an extra step after leaving the counter: open a web page and type in a code. Older shoppers were unfamiliar with it, shoppers in a hurry skipped it, and codes were easily lost once out of the store.

05 Recommendations for the Next Cycle

  1. Segment stores by performance and set targets accordingly

    Group outlets into three tiers by last cycle's participation rate (above 80% / 50–80% / below 50%), set an appropriate target for each and run a dedicated support programme for the lowest tier.

  2. Widen the qualifying condition

    Qualify on total receipt value rather than a single category, or combine two to three core categories at once, to widen the eligible customer base.

  3. Cut the steps required of the customer

    Print a QR code on the receipt that opens the game directly, so customers can scan at the counter instead of memorising a code and finding the site later.

Key Takeaways

This is the one case where I worked with an external technology vendor end to end: sourcing them, negotiating terms, then reconciling the data they returned:

  • Vendor management: selecting a partner for something the chain had never done before, negotiating cost and terms, and owning the outcome.
  • Reading data from a digital platform: taking figures returned by a third-party system and reconciling them down to store level to find the cause, rather than reporting the headline number.
  • Spotting the bottleneck in the customer journey: pinpointing the drop-off at the hand-off from checkout to phone — exactly where offline-to-online programmes tend to lose people.
